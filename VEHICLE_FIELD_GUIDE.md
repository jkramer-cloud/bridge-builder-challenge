## Masses versus bridge difficulty

The **crossing load** represents a plausible educational mass estimate. The **bridge strength requirement** grows steadily with each level but is normalized for playability. The game does not calculate a certified live load, axle stress, deflection, safety factor, structural certification, or a real bridge design. Even if a scenario visually crosses the river, real vehicles such as trains and NASA crawler transporters operate on purpose-built infrastructure.

## Sources for selected real-world examples

- [2025 Ford Mustang GT technical specifications](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/content/dam/fordmediasite/us/en/library/2025/specs/2025_Mustang_Technical_Specs.pdf): estimated GT fastback manual curb weight **3,827 lb**; the classroom mission adds a representative driver to that curb mass.
- [2025 Ford F-150 technical specifications](https://f150europe.com/-/media/project/hedin/navigo/f150europesite/pdf/pdf_my25/f150-technical-specification-my25-eng.pdf?rev=91ea9f2b91154a27a56c71f14b16de54): Lariat V8 curb weight approx. **2,303 kg**, which is combined with a representative driver for the mission.
- [NASA — Crawler Transporter](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/exploration-ground-systems/the-crawlers/): crawler mass approx. **6.6 million lb**, carrying capacity up to **18 million lb**.
- [NASA — Mobile Launcher 1](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/exploration-ground-systems/mobile-launcher/): mobile launcher mass approx. **11.5 million lb**, making a crawler-plus-launcher combination around **18.1 million lb** before other payloads.

All other entries are representative or fictional scenario loads rather than specifications for an exact make/model. Extreme scenarios are deliberately fanciful and should not be taken as recommended transport practices.

## Suggested student comparisons

1. Compare two bicycle modes: how does adding a rider or cargo affect total crossing load?
2. Compare a sport motorcycle and a Mustang GT: how many times more weight does the bridge carry?
3. Compare a loaded highway semi and a triple-trailer log convoy: why are axle arrangement and road limits important even when total mass is known?
4. Compare NASA’s crawler weight with its payload capability: why does a special-purpose crawlerway matter?
5. Evaluate the simulation: how are the teaching-model stress percentages different from a real engineer’s calculated forces?
